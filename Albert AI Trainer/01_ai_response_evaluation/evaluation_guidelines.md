# AI Response Evaluation Guidelines

## Purpose
This independent practice project demonstrates a repeatable process for assessing AI-generated answers. The examples are educational demonstrations, not paid client work or a claim of professional annotation experience.

## Scoring scale
- 1 — Very poor: major failure or serious error.
- 2 — Poor: substantial weakness.
- 3 — Acceptable: mixed quality or noticeable issues.
- 4 — Good: minor issue(s).
- 5 — Excellent: meets the criterion well.

## Criteria
1. Accuracy: factual and logical correctness.
2. Relevance: how directly the response addresses the prompt.
3. Instruction following: whether requested format, length, audience, and constraints are followed.
4. Clarity: readability and organization.
5. Completeness: whether enough of the requested information is provided.

## Evaluation procedure
1. Read the prompt and identify explicit constraints.
2. Check factual claims and calculations.
3. Assess each criterion independently.
4. Write a specific justification, not just a score.
5. Provide a corrected response when needed.
6. Flag serious safety or factual errors even if other criteria score well.

## Important caveats
- The simple arithmetic mean is a summary, not a universal quality measure.
- Serious safety or factual errors may override a high average.
- Verify claims with reliable sources when the task requires external factual validation.
- Treat ambiguous prompts consistently and document assumptions.
- These examples are synthetic practice samples. Scores are illustrative judgments and should be reviewed critically.
